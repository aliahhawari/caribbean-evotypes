ABOUT PROSTATE CANCER

What is prostate cancer?

The prostate is a small gland that sits below the bladder and surrounds the tube that carries urine out of the body. Prostate cancer develops when cells in the prostate begin to grow in an uncontrolled way.

Prostate cancers do not all behave in the same way. Some grow slowly and may never cause problems, while others can grow more quickly and may need treatment.

This page gives a short introduction only. Use the trusted resources below for detailed and up-to-date health information.

WHY THIS MATTERS FOR BLACK MEN

Black men have a higher risk of prostate cancer

In the UK, Prostate Cancer UK estimates that 1 in 4 Black men will be diagnosed with prostate cancer in their lifetime. Age and family history also affect risk.

RISK & SYMPTOMS

Understanding your risk

Prostate cancer can affect anyone with a prostate, but some people have a higher risk. Age, ethnicity and family history are important factors to discuss with a healthcare professional.

01

Age

The risk of prostate cancer increases as you get older.

02

Black ethnicity

Black men have a higher lifetime risk of developing prostate cancer.

03

Family history

Your risk may be higher if close relatives have had prostate cancer.

Prostate cancer often has no symptoms in its early stages.

Urinary symptoms can also be caused by common non-cancerous prostate conditions. If you are concerned about symptoms or your risk, speak to your GP or another healthcare professional.

PSA TESTING

What is a PSA test?

A PSA test is a blood test that measures the level of prostate-specific antigen (PSA) in your blood. A raised PSA can have several causes and does not automatically mean that you have prostate cancer.

What can affect PSA?

PSA levels can be affected by prostate cancer as well as non-cancerous prostate conditions and other factors.

Should I have a PSA test?

The benefits and limitations of PSA testing are worth discussing with a healthcare professional, particularly if you are at increased risk.

TESTS & DIAGNOSIS

How is prostate cancer investigated?

If prostate cancer is suspected, healthcare professionals may use a combination of tests and examinations. Which tests are needed depends on your individual circumstances.

1

Initial assessment

Your doctor may discuss your symptoms, risk and general health.

2

PSA blood test

A blood test can measure the amount of PSA in your blood.

3

Examination

A clinician may discuss or perform a prostate examination depending on your circumstances.

4

Further tests

Further assessment can include MRI and, where appropriate, a prostate biopsy.

TREATMENT

Treatment for prostate cancer

There are different ways to manage and treat prostate cancer. Treatment depends on factors including the type and stage of the cancer, your general health and your preferences.

Monitoring

Some prostate cancers can be monitored rather than treated immediately.

Surgery

Surgery may be an option for some people with prostate cancer.

Radiotherapy

Radiotherapy uses radiation to destroy cancer cells.

Drug treatments

Hormone therapy, chemotherapy and other medicines may be used in some situations.

Your treatment is individual to you.

Your clinical team can explain which options are appropriate for you and discuss the potential benefits and side effects.
